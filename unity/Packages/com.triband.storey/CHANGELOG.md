# Changelog

All notable changes to this package are documented here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/) and the package adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/) as Unity defines it (MAJOR = breaking public API or data format, MINOR = additions, PATCH = fixes). Data format version: see `StoreyVersion.DataFormat`.

## [Unreleased]

### Changed
- In Play mode, loading over frames, a site's layout and its neighbours' are parsed on a build thread (`StoreyDocumentAsset.DocumentOffMainThread`, `Lod.LayoutParse`), and the site is shown the frame they are ready. The parse held the frame a site started in, 0.1–0.2 s and 60 MB of garbage for the 3,000-building test city (measured headless), so a district streamed in made a hitch. The site is `Loading` meanwhile, and its `PlanArea` is null until its layout is parsed (asking for it no longer parses it). Edit mode, and Play mode with **Load Over Frames** off, parse at once as before. `StoreyDocumentAsset.Parsed` gives the layout only if it is parsed already.
- A layout loads in about a third of the frames. Its cells' massings are built four a build thread ahead of the cell taken (`LayoutLoad.AheadPerThread`), where one a thread left the threads idle most of the load: each frame took only the few cells that were ready. Measured headless on the 3,000-building test city, every cell shows after 0.9–1.1 s with two build threads at 60 fps (was 2.4–2.5 s) and 1.2–1.3 s at 30 fps (was 4.5–4.6 s); the first view's detail is done after 1.6–1.7 s (was 2.4–2.5 and 4.5–4.6 s). A load's frames now spend up to the build budget on it (**Build Budget Ms**), where they spent 1–3 ms. The massings in flight hold no measurable memory more. `LayoutLoad.InFlight` counts them.
- Storey keeps no state per thread. Unity's manual warns that the .NET `[ThreadStatic]` attribute crashes a Unity script, and the build threads' spare mesh builders, wall-panel and weld lists, triangulator nodes and mesh packer were thread-static, as were the walk model's query lists. A mesh builder now keeps its own panel and weld lists (one thread uses it at a time) and its spares are shared by every thread (16 kept, where each thread kept 6); the triangulator's arrays and nodes (`TriScratch`, an `Earcut` instance), a mesh's packer and the walk model's query lists are lent for each call and given back, a lock each way. `Earcut` is a class whose instance `Tessellate` reuses its nodes; the static one makes an instance. The same meshes, the same 91 MB of garbage on the five-minute walk and the same build times; Storey holds 0.1 MB more after the walk. `PackageLayoutTests` fails if package code declares a `[ThreadStatic]` or `[ContextStatic]` field or uses `ThreadLocal<T>`.
- Detail builds make almost no garbage of their own. The wall panels, their triangulation (Earcut) and the openings they merge use their builder's own lists, nodes and copies again for every wall (`Triangulate.Shape`'s list overload, `TriScratch`, `Earcut`'s instance `Tessellate`, `Opening.Set`); each wall's openings and panel options are made again wall after wall (`Facade.Ops`' list overload with an `OpeningPool`, `Facade.PanelOpt.Set`); four-cornered polygons are drawn without an array (`MeshBuilder.Quad`); and the details' hash no longer boxes its numbers (`Details.Hash01`'s typed overload). The same meshes. The party-wall memo is keyed by numbers instead of text, and the details work out a storey's corners once instead of once an edge. With the chunks above, the five-minute walk of the 3,000-building city makes 91 MB of garbage instead of 2,818, a sixth of it the chunks' first fill (32 MB in its busiest second, the first builds, instead of 376; 0.3 MB in a typical busy second), holding 16.6 MB for it; a building's detail build takes 15–18% less time on a worker, and its LOD0 alone allocates 1.8–2.6 MB instead of 13–18 on the test cities.
- Buildings that nothing can hide draw without discard. The opaque and massing shaders have a steady variant (`#pragma multi_compile_local _ STOREY_STEADY` in every pass: no discard, no Sink), and the renderer switches each building's opaque and massing renderers, and each cell's, to a copy of the material with it on while the building isn't cross-fading, isolated against, in the way, or the player's (or carrying a party wall with it): `Occlusion.Steady` holds the rule, `PackedMesh.Mates` the party-wall neighbours, `StoreyGlobals.ActiveIndex`, `IsolateAmount` and `IsolatedIndex` the view. Apple's GPUs can then hide most of the city's pixels before shading them; how much that saves needs Xcode's GPU counters on a device. A project shader without the variant keeps one material and draws as before. `StoreyQualitySettings.occlusionOnFarMaterials`, which did nothing, now keeps massings in the way steady when off (`LodSettings.FarOcclusion`).
- Colliders are cooked without holding a frame: a batch is scheduled on Unity's job workers (`Physics.BakeMesh` in an `IJobParallelFor`) and its colliders are assigned in a later frame, once it has finished, where the main thread used to wait for the whole batch in the frame that started it. A building released or rebuilt meanwhile has its collision mesh destroyed only after the batch; edit mode still finishes the batch at once.
- Detail and massing builds run on Storey's own build threads (`BuildThreads`, a `TaskScheduler`) instead of the .NET thread pool: long-lived background threads named "Storey build N", below normal priority where the platform allows it, and registered with Unity's Profiler, where `Storey.Generate LOD0`, `Storey.Generate LOD1`, `Storey.Pack` and `Storey.Collision mesh` markers show their work. How many is `StoreyQualitySettings`' new **Build Threads**; automatic (0) is two on a phone or tablet and one fewer than the cores, at most four, on a desktop, where it was one fewer than the cores everywhere, as many as Unity's own job workers. The threads end when the player quits and before the editor reloads scripts. `StoreyTimings`' stages are `ProfilerMarker`s.
- Detail builds reuse their memory. A mesh builder's lists and a packed mesh are made of fixed-size chunks (`Chunks`, `ChunkList<T>`: 2,048 elements): a list grows a chunk at a time without copying, and gives its chunks back when its builder is recycled (`MeshBuilder.Make` and `Recycle`) or its mesh uploaded (`PackedMesh.Release`), and any chunk fits the next build whatever the sizes of the two buildings. A mesh past 65,535 vertices is split into parts without a whole-mesh copy. The same meshes byte for byte. The chunks kept between builds are capped by `StoreyQualitySettings`' new **Mesh Chunks Kept MB** (16; 12 on the minimum tier), each type of chunk to its share of what builds take, so a builder's lists, given back first, can't fill the cap and leave its packed mesh's none. A builder holds a vertex's normal and colour as indices into its own tables of them (`IndexedList`, `MeshBuilder.N` and `C`: 4 bytes each instead of 24), so its lists take 46 bytes a vertex instead of 86, and the largest builds need a third fewer chunks. `MeshBuilder`'s lists and `PackedMesh.V`, `Cut` and `I16` are `ChunkList`s, uploaded a chunk at a time; `Site` no longer records each building's last size.
- LOD0 and its glass are packed without welding (`MeshPack.Pack`'s `weld`): the generator shares their vertices already, and welding found 0.27% more on the test cities while taking a fifth to a quarter of a worker's build. A LOD0 build takes 20% less worker time on the 3,000-building test city (6.6 ms, was 8.2) and 27% less on the walk-in one (12.2 ms, was 16.6). LOD1 is still welded (3.7% fewer vertices). `WeldTests` fails if the generator starts making duplicates.
- The building table uploads only the chunks that changed each frame (`DirtyChunks`): on the five-minute walk of the 3,000-building test city, 1.0 KB a frame on average where the whole wall, state and occluder buffers went up every frame (344 KB). `BuildingTable.SetState`, `SetWall`, `SetWallData` and `MarkOccDirty(slot)`; writing straight into the arrays still works with the `Mark…Dirty()` calls, which upload the whole buffer. The wall tables start at 8,192 wall ids and double when full, up to 65,536 (`WallIdsStart`): 2.2 MB less in all.
- The automatic LOD passes over squares of buildings that can't change: it keeps them in 128 m squares, and a square whose buildings all show their massing (or nothing), with neither its nearest nor its farthest point crossing a threshold, is skipped whole. The picks are exactly those of looking at every building (`LodSquaresTests`); on the 3,000-building test city a frame takes 46 µs, not 157 (Release). Ties in the build and drop lists now go in building order. The caps look only at the buildings holding meshes.
- The far city's cells store each massing's facade numbers (metres along a wall and up a run, the window span, the bay's width, the kind) as 16-bit floats: 36 bytes a vertex instead of 48, 9.8 MB of GPU memory instead of 12.8 on the 3,000-building test city. The shader reads them as it did; they are 2.5 cm off at most on the test cities, a tenth of a pixel where a massing shows. A GPU that can't read 16-bit floats in a vertex (`SystemInfo.SupportsVertexAttributeFormat`) gets the 48-byte vertex. `MeshUpload.MassVertexHalf`, `MeshPack.Half`.
- A layout's massings are built on the worker threads, a few cells ahead of the main thread, which meanwhile writes and merges the cells before: on the 3,000-building test city the main thread works about 50 ms of the load instead of about 300 ms (headless, 3 workers). `LayoutLoad`.
- A building's collision mesh is made on the worker thread with its first LOD0, not on the main thread (0.4 ms a building on average on the test city, 1.9 ms in the walk-in one).
- A building built again (its LOD0 or LOD1 dropped and wanted back) is made at the size of its last build, without growing its lists: LOD0 and LOD1 together allocate 4.8 MB, not 8.7 (10.5, not 18, in the walk-in city). `MeshBuilder`'s `vertices` and `indices`.
- Detail meshes are packed into the upload's vertex format on the worker thread that built them (`MeshPack`, `PackedMesh`). The main thread only fills in each vertex's colour row and wall id and copies: 0.13 ms a building on the 3,000-building test city, was 2.5 ms (worst 42). A finished build waits for its upload at 0.8 MB, not 3.0 (1.7, not 6.5, for a walk-in building). `MeshUpload.Upload(PackedMesh, …)`; `MeshUpload.Vertex` and `Cutaway` are now `PackedMesh.Vertex` and `Cutaway`.
- A LOD0 mesh past 65,535 vertices comes in parts with 16-bit indices, not as one mesh with 32-bit ones. The test city's 16 largest LOD0s take 8% less GPU memory (90.5 MB, was 98.6), in 51 meshes instead of 32.
- Building and welding a LOD0 allocates half as much: 14 MB instead of 29 for a building of the walk-in test city, on average. `MeshBuilder.W`, a copy of each vertex's wall data that nothing read since the walls' data moved into the building table, is gone; `MeshBuilder.Weld` works in place with a table of vertex indices instead of a dictionary of copied keys; boxes add their faces with `MeshBuilder.Quad`, without an array each. The meshes are the same to the bit.
- What is kept of a building's LOD0 after upload is its shape (`Lod0Shape`: the collision segments and the wall list), not the generator's whole result. `SiteRenderer.ShapeOf` and `StoreySite.BuiltShape` replace `Lod0Of` and `BuiltLod0`; `CollisionMesh.Build` takes a shape; `Lod0Result.Shape`. The walk model's `ShapeOf`, `Use`, `ShapeCount` and `ShapesBuilt` replace `Lod0Of`, `UseLod0` and `Lod0Count` (docs/CITY.md §7).
- LOD1's walls are flat: a plain window is an opaque pane 3 cm proud of the wall (`Lod1.PaneProud`), its reveal shaded in the shader at the pane's sides and head (`StoreyRevealShade`), instead of a hole cut in the wall with modelled reveals. Doors and artist-made windows keep their openings. On the 3,000-building test city LOD1 goes from about 4,230 triangles a building to 980. `Lod1.Build(..., recessed: true)` builds the prototype's LOD1 for the parity tests.
- Every object a Storey Site makes (buildings, their LOD objects and colliders, the merged cells, Sink's footprints) is on the site's own layer, not Default; changing the site's layer moves them. `SiteRenderer.Layer`.
- `StoreyQualitySettings`' level-of-detail fields are now the automatic LOD's numbers (`lod0PixelsPerMetre`, `lod1PixelsPerMetre`, `lod2PixelsPerMetre`, `hysteresis`, `fadeSeconds`, `farDistance`, `lod0Residency`, `lod1Residency`, `buildBudgetMs`), with defaults from the prototype. `ToLodSettings`, `ApplyTo`.
- Cut corners are kept as one corner: `CornerData` (the building's and a setback's `corners`), and `CornerCuts` (`Sharp`, `Around`, `Cut`, `Clear`, `CutAll`, `ClearAll`). The outline still holds the cut's points. `CornerShape` moved to the `Triband.Storey` namespace.
- LOD0 vertices are 28 bytes instead of 44. They carry only the cutaway kind and wall id; each wall's start and normal sit once in the building table (`_StoreyWallData`), and the shader reads them by id. On the demo street LOD0 goes from 4.6 to 3.1 MB.
- `MeshUpload.Upload` welds exact duplicate vertices (`MeshBuilder.Weld`, about 1% of LOD0's; every triangle is unchanged), and calls `Mesh.Optimize` when asked. `SiteRenderer` asks in Play mode, not for the editor's previews.
- `StoreyGlobals.SetOcclusion` takes the player's chest (world space) and the hole radius in metres. The shader projects the player itself, so Cutout and Fade hold on reversed-depth platforms and flipped render targets.
- A building's row in the building table is its index in the layout, so party walls find their neighbour in the shader.

### Removed
- `StoreyQualitySettings.gpuOcclusionCulling` and `targetFrameRate`. Nothing read them: Unity has no per-renderer switch for GPU occlusion culling (it is each quality level's Universal Renderer setting), the frame rate is the project's (`Application.targetFrameRate`), and Storey's own budget is `buildBudgetMs`. docs/SPEC.md §6.7 lists the project settings Storey needs, Depth Priming off among them.

### Fixed
- An edit's problem check compared every pair of buildings for overlaps, however few buildings it checked: 4.5 million pairs on the 3,000-building test city. It takes the candidates from the site's grid (`Site.InBox`), in the same order: checking an edited building and its neighbours there takes about 70 ms instead of about 320.
- Memory: Storey kept the generator's working data after the meshes were uploaded, at 4.7 to 5 times the meshes' size. On the 3,000-building test city after a five-minute walk that was 277 MB of C# memory never read again, beside 45 MB of GPU meshes. It now keeps none of it: 53 MB in all, with the smaller cells, LOD0 parts and wall tables under Changed (headless .NET; docs/CITY.md §7).
  - The renderer kept each LOD0 building's whole generator result (3.5 to 7.7 MB a building, 32 MB for a large tower). It keeps the building's shape, 10–25 KB.
  - Every building's massing was kept to merge its cell again (50 MB). A layout now loads cell by cell, letting each cell's massings go once it is merged; a cell merged again makes its buildings' massings again (about 1 ms for the biggest).
  - The walk model built a whole LOD0 for every building the player came near, on the main thread, and kept it until the layout changed: 170 MB and frames of up to 68 ms on that walk. It now takes the renderer's shapes (`PlayWorld.Source`, set by the Storey Occlusion), builds only a shape when there's none (`Lod0.BuildShape`, about 50 times faster than LOD0), and keeps at most `PlayWorld.Keep` (32): 1.4 MB, with a worst frame of 1 ms.
- A building that had to have LOD0 at once (the player's, one in the camera's way) while a worker was already building it was built a second time on the main thread. The main thread now waits for the worker's build.
- A detail build that failed on a worker thread was dropped without a word. It's logged now, unless the layout changed under it.
- The problem list built every interior building's whole LOD0 for its walls. It builds shapes: the 3,000-building test city's full check takes 0.7 s instead of 2.7 s.
- No buildings in an iOS (IL2CPP) build with Color Pipeline installed: managed code stripping removed the Color Pipeline bridge, which nothing references, so every site threw "Storey's bridge did not compile". The bridge is now `[assembly: AlwaysLinkAssembly]` and registers its palette at startup (`StoreyPalettes.RegisterBridge`); the error in a player names stripping.
- Garbage every frame in the automatic LOD's cell check: it enumerated each far cell's members, a `SortedDictionary`, whose enumerator allocates. It now walks a plain array of the members made when the cell is built. The Profiler sample names are cached in a plain dictionary.
- About 14 KB of garbage a frame in the automatic LOD while moving: the lambda that starts a worker job captured the build loop's locals, so its closure was made on every pass over the buildings wanting detail, job or not. The job starts in its own method, and the loop stops once every thread is busy.
- Garbage every frame in Play mode, over 100 KB a frame moving through a big city. The occlusion core tested the buildings near the camera's sightline by building each pitched roof again to read its rise, making tier lists, and `Tiers.Hypot` made an array on every call; the walk model made lists in every grid lookup, copied a setback's style to read its roof type on the stairs, and made a list of walls per collision; the LOD manager made its frame, its build list and its eviction list each frame; the site built a string of its neighbours, a new delegate for the occlusion and a new LOD array each frame; every Profiler sample joined its name. Walking, the occlusion core and the LOD pick now make none after warm-up (`FrameAllocTests`, on the 3,000-building city and the prototype's walks indoors). `LodManager.Frame` is now the manager's own, valid until the next `Update`. `PlayWorld.NearInto`, `LodManager.Resident`, `Derived.ExteriorStyleAt`.
- A long pause the first time the player walked into a building in Play mode: the cutaway's check for shared walls built every building's LOD0 for the walk model. It now looks only at the buildings next to the player's.
- A long pause the first time the Storey Site was selected (in Play mode too): starting the edit built every building again, though the layout was the one shown.
- Compiler warnings in Unity:
  - `Physics.BakeMesh(int, …)` obsolete in Unity 6.3: the warning is suppressed there, since the call still works.
  - `StoreyWindows.light` hid `Component.light`: now marked `new`.
  - Nullable annotations in Earcut and Clipper outside a `#nullable` context: those files now enable annotations only.
- A straight flight against the building's wall flickered at every storey's slab. The side of the slab's opening lay in the wall's inner face, because the walls run down through the slab. That side is no longer drawn for a straight flight's opening along the outline, and the steps leave out their side against the wall. (Switchback stairs and atria keep the prototype's faces.)
- Straight flights that came up onto a setback's terrace ran up under a closed deck. The terrace deck is now cut round a stair opening on it, and the floor above the setback leaves it out. The walk model and the collision mesh already had the opening.
- Gable and shed roofs on buildings with rounded or chamfered corners looked broken: each of a cut corner's short edges became an eave or a hip of its own. Their planes are now planned on the sharp outline (`CornerCuts.SharpMap`, `Roofs.Make`'s `sharp`) and fitted to the cut one. A cut corner's edges take the part of the side they turn nearer to, so the eave runs round the corner and the gable wall follows it. Hip and mansard roofs still round with the corners. Where two nearly parallel edges stand out by different amounts, the roof's edge no longer spikes out.
- Every edit rewrote every building's colour rows. With Color Pipeline that took 0.9 s an edit on the 300-building test city, because every palette lookup walks the palette twice. An edit now retargets the colour rows without rewriting them (`ColorRowBook.Retarget(resolver, rewrite: false)`); only the rebuilt buildings' rows are written. `ColorPipelinePalette.IndexOf` remembers each colour reference's index, checking it against the palette before use.
- In a big layout, most buildings' LOD2 massings showed another building's colours, windows and storey heights, so switching to LOD1 or LOD0 changed their look. The building table's massing parameters were a fixed 512 rows, and every row past the last was written into the last one: the 3,000-building test city needs about 9,000. The parameter and colour-row tables now start at 1,024 rows and double when full (`BuildingTable.ParamRowsStart`, `ColorRowsStart`).
- Entering Play mode crashed the editor while the scene loaded, inside `Mesh.Optimize`'s vertex-cache pass, on valid LOD0 meshes. `MeshUpload` no longer calls `Mesh.Optimize`: the generator's triangle order is within a few percent of a vertex-cache optimiser's. Empty detail meshes (a shell building's LOD0 glass) are no longer uploaded. The upload checks every index, so a bad one is a managed exception naming the mesh rather than a native crash.
- The Massing shader's depth and shadow passes failed to compile ("undeclared identifier 'StoreyWindowId'"). `StoreyFacade.hlsl` now includes the new `StoreyWindowId.hlsl` itself.
- Color Palette Lit sample: its three shaders include `StoreyColorPaletteLit.hlsl` with `#include_with_pragmas`, so that file's own `#include_with_pragmas` lines are honoured. This removes the "Treating '#include_with_pragmas' directive as '#include'" and "'multi_compile_fog': unknown pragma ignored" warnings. Re-import the sample to get it.
- Dormer windows ignored the style's window rules. They're now dressed as the facade's windows are (`Facade.Dress`: sills, heads, frames, bars, the artist's window, the window shader's rooms), through `RoofPart.WinF`/`WinO`.
- The Color Pipeline palette no longer logs a warning for a colour missing from the palette, or for one shown as its nearest match.
- The editor's cutaway in the Interior tab. Every wall has an id, so the shader drops walls by their slide values, which only the occlusion system set (in Play mode). The site's renderer now drops the walls between the Scene view camera and the focus itself.
- The cutaway on a Storey Site moved away from the origin, in the editor and in play. The camera and focus are now given in the site's own x and z.

### Added
- Loading over frames: a site's first load in Play mode (a district streamed in, a scene starting) goes over several frames, nearest cells first, within the build budget, so it never holds a frame. After an edit, and in the editor, a load is done at once. `SiteRenderer.LoadOverFrames` and `Loading`, `StoreySite.LoadOverFrames` and `Loading`, `LodStats.loading`.
- `MemoryBenchTests`: what a building keeps after its upload, what a finished build holds, what building allocates, a load's massings and the walk model on a five-minute walk, on the 3,000-building test city, with bounds that catch a regression (docs/CITY.md §7).
- `Lod0.BuildShape`: a building's LOD0 shape without its meshes, exactly the full build's segments and walls (`MeshBuilder.ShapeOnly`). `PlayWorld.Source` and `Keep`. `Lod0ShapeTests`.
- Props (docs/PROPS.md): objects inside buildings occluded with them. **Storey Prop** (any object or prefab root) files its renderers under the building they're in and writes the building's table index into their Renderer Shader User Value; a project's Shader Graph reads it through `StoreyProp.hlsl`'s `StoreyPropVertex` and `StoreyPropFragment` Custom Function nodes and goes through the buildings' own `StoreySink` and `StoreyOcclude`. Moving props (rigidbodies) are found again as they go. A building's props are switched off once it settles at LOD1 or LOD2. **Hide Whole** switches a prop off for shaders that can't take the nodes (`OcclusionCore.HidesPoint`). `PropLocator`, `Site.InCell`, `SiteRenderer.Version`, `AnyVersion` and `DetailShown`.
- Buildings at different heights: `BuildingData.elevation` (the ground floor's height in the site; saved as `elevation` only when raised). `Derived.FloorBase` and `RoofY` are site heights, so the generator, the walk model, the occlusion and bridges follow it. Shared walls between neighbours at different heights cover only the heights both reach (`Party.Range.Lo`, `Party.Shares`); each building's storeys outside that band keep their own outside wall. The plinth and foundation stand on the building's ground floor. `Ground.Samples` and `Ground.Snap` stand a building on uneven ground: on the highest point, its foundation down past the lowest. A building made from a template starts at elevation 0.
- LOD0's see-through windows start as LOD1's painted panes where LOD0 takes over and clear to glass closer in, so the switch to LOD0 no longer changes every window at once (`StoreyQualitySettings.windowsClearAt`, `LodSettings.GlassClear` and `Lod0Distance`, `StoreyGlobals.SetGlassFill`, `_StoreyGlassFill`). LOD0's glass panes now carry their place on the pane, as the opaque ones do.
- `OcclusionSettings.wallMargin` and `holdAfterExit` (and on `StoreyOcclusionSettings`): a wall the cutaway drops waits for the player to be 0.3 m past it, and a building the player steps out of stays cut away while it is still in the camera's way. `PlayWorld.Touching`. `StoreySite.Preview` with the text the edit was read from.
- Dissolve, a buildings-in-the-way mode (`OccluderMode.Dissolve`, `StoreyGlobals.OcclusionMode.Dissolve`): Sink's end state, reached by fading instead of collapsing. The footprint fades in as the building fades out, on complementary dither patterns, and the shadow fades with it. Each occluder slot's footprint has its own row in the building table (`BuildingTable.FootprintRow`), so loaded sites can now have 8,176 buildings between them (`BuildingTable.Capacity`), not 8,191.
- Districts (docs/CITY.md §5):
  - `BuildingTable.Acquire`, `ReleaseShared`, `AllocBlock` and `ReleaseBlock`: one building table shared by every Storey Site, each with a block of building indices (`IndexBlocks`, `MateIndex`). `MeshUpload.Upload`'s `mates`.
  - Only the site with a view writes the view globals.
  - `StoreySite.neighbours` (`NeighbourDistrict`) and `PlanArea`; `Site.WithContext`, `Own` and `OwnerKey`: party walls across district edges, built once.
  - `StoreyOcclusion.followPlayer`: the occlusion moves to the district the player is in (`Districts.PlayerIn`).
  - `StoreyDistricts`: district scenes loaded and unloaded by distance (`Districts.Plan`).
- `TestCity.Generate(doc, count, walkIn)`: the same city with every building walk-in, with stairs to the top and a lift above five storeys.
- `Walls.Inside` and the problem list's "wall-outside": a wall wholly outside its storey (over a terrace) builds nothing, so it is flagged.
- `StoreyTimings`: Profiler samples and per-stage times for an edit's work (docs/EDITOR.md §2.1).
- `EditSession.Restore`: put one building back without reading the whole layout (a drag step). `EditSession.Discard` only reads back the buildings an operation changed, and the commit finds bridge links in one pass, not one per building. On the 3,000-building city: a commit goes from about 350 to 140 ms, a drag step from about 1.4 s to 0.1 s.
- `Problems.Check` with a scope, `Problems.Scope`, `Changed`, `Prints` and `Merge`: check only what an edit touched. `Problem.otherId`. The overlap test skips footprints whose bounding boxes don't overlap: the 3,000-building city's full check goes from 22 s to 7 s.
- City scale (docs/CITY.md):
  - `TestCity`, the prototype's test city, building for building.
  - `LodManager` and `LodSettings`, the automatic LOD.
  - `Cells`, LOD2 massings merged into 128 m cells. `Lod2Mesh.Tags` holds per-vertex tags for merged meshes.
  - `SiteRenderer` takes `LodSettings` for the automatic LOD, with `Eye`, `Forced` and `Stats`.
  - `StoreySite.lodMode` (Automatic by default), `lodCamera`, `quality`, `showLodStats`, `keepDetail`, `LodStats`, `EditorCamera` and `All`.
  - `SiteView.focusId`.
  - LOD0 and LOD1 are generated on worker threads (`LodSettings.Threads`, `StoreyQualitySettings.buildOnWorkerThreads`); the main thread only uploads them. The generator's memos (`Site`) and caches (`Colors`, `OpeningKinds`) are thread-safe. `MeshBuilder.Welded`.
- `FacadeStyle.foundationOut` (layout key `foundationOut`; null = 0.08 m): how far the foundation stands out from the wall. `Lod0.FoundationOut`.
- `FacadeStyle.sills` and `heads` (layout keys `sills`, `heads`; on by default): a window's sill and its head (flat or arched) can be left out.
- Artist-made windows and street doors (docs/EDITOR.md §6.9). Engine-free: `OpeningKind` and `OpeningKinds`, plus `FacadeStyle.windowKind` and `doorKind`. Unity: the `StoreyOpening` asset (*Create › Storey › Window or Door*) and `StoreySite.openings`. `MeshBuilder.Mesh` takes raw triangles; `Facade.PaneUV` is split out of `Facade.Pane`.
- Windows with rooms behind them (docs/EDITOR.md §6.8), after the project's Window shader graph. Add the `StoreyWindows` component and give it a room atlas.
  - Opaque panes in LOD0 and LOD1 carry their place on the pane and the window's number (`MeshBuilder.PaneUV`, `ColorRows.EncodePane`, `Facade.WindowId`).
  - LOD2's painted windows work out the same number, so a window keeps its room and its light across an LOD switch.

- Bridges between buildings (`BuildingData.bridges`, `BridgeData`, `Bridges`, `BridgeEdits`; docs/EDITOR.md §6.4). A bridge goes straight out from one building's wall to the facing wall of another, with a door at each end. It's enclosed (glass and a roof) or open (rails), and ramps between floors at different heights. Every LOD, the walk model (`Lod0Result.Extra` holds its sides) and the colliders include it. `EditSession` rebuilds both ends. Deleting a building removes the bridges to it, and `Opening.bare` drops the canopy over a bridge's doors.
- Courtyards and atria (`BuildingData.voids`, `VoidData`, `Courtyards`, `Voids`; docs/EDITOR.md §6.3). A courtyard has facades facing into it, a door onto it and paving, with a parapet round it on a flat roof or a light well through a pitched one. An atrium cuts the floors above its bottom, behind rails, under a skylight. Every LOD, the walk model and the colliders follow them. Outlines, cores and presets keep clear of them (`OutlineIssue.Void`). `Facade.Dress` is factored out of the LOD0 facade.

- Mesh colliders (docs/PLAY.md §5). `CollisionMesh` is a building's floors, stairs, walls and pitched roofs, built from the walk model's data and tested against it. `SiteRenderer.Colliders` gives each building a `MeshCollider`, cooked in parallel with `Physics.BakeMesh` and assigned already cooked. The switches are `StoreySite.generateColliders` (on) and `collidersInEditMode` (off). The package now depends on `com.unity.modules.physics`.

- `SiteView.isolateAmount`: an isolate that fades, rather than switching at once.
- `MeshBuilder.WallBase`: the floor height of the storey each wall was made on. `SiteRenderer` uses it to slide only the active storey's walls in the editor, eased, and reports `Animating` (also on `StoreySite`) while they move.
- Filled storeys (`FloorData.filled`, `"filled": true` in a layout; walk-in buildings). The storey is built as a shell-only building's storeys are, with opaque windows, closed doors, and no rooms, stairs or slabs between filled storeys. `Cores.Stops` gives a core's storeys without the filled ones: lifts pass through those without stopping, stairs stop at them (`HasFlight`, `StairHoleAt`), and the storeys on either side rail the dead end. Layouts without filled storeys build as before.
- `StoreyOcclusion` and `StoreyOcclusionSettings` (docs/PLAY.md, slice 5.3; stub-compiled, not yet run in an editor). - Play and occlusion, engine-free (docs/PLAY.md, slices 5.1 and 5.2). `Play/PlayWorld` locates the player and gives the surface underfoot, including both kinds of stairs, and collision against LOD0's wall segments. `Walker` handles walking, running, gravity and lifts, and `FollowCamera` the follow camera. `Occlusion/OcclusionCore` computes the view, the sliding cutaway, and buildings in the way, with the three rays, the door rule, fade and hold, slots, Slice, Sink's plan and rows, and camera assist. - `Shafts.Slide`, the editor's core drag: where `Drag` refuses (the pointer is past a wall or on another core), the core goes as far as it fits along each of its own axes, so it slides along what stops it.
- Straight flights (`CoreType.Flight`, `"flight"` in a layout): stairs over the same floor range as switchback stairs, with one straight flight per storey stacked above the last, a walkway beside it back to the next flight, and no walls. Each flight has risers of about 18 cm, and the slab is open over the flight lane's run only. Rails run along the opening, the roof's included: there is no bulkhead, and the parapet runs on past them. `Shafts.SetKind` switches stairs between the two kinds where the other fits. Storey's own: the prototype cannot read a layout that has one. Layouts without them read and write as before.
