# Changelog

All notable changes to this package are documented here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/) and the package adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/) as Unity defines it (MAJOR = breaking public API or data format, MINOR = additions, PATCH = fixes). Data format version: see `StoreyVersion.DataFormat`.

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **The selection outline on the building being edited alone** (docs/EDITOR.md §2, `SelectionOutlineTests`): with the site selected, Unity outlined every building, the site's children. In the Storey context only the building being edited is outlined (none while none is picked); in the GameObject context the whole site still is. Each Storey shader has a selection pass (`SceneSelectionPass`, `STOREY_SELECTION`, `StoreySelectionFrag`), compiled when first drawn, which keeps the building `_StoreyOutline` names (`StoreyShaderIds.Outline`, set by the editor's `StoreySelectionOutline`; 0, its default, keeps every building), so it works under the GPU Resident Drawer and in merged cells. A player never draws it. A project's own Storey shader adds the same pass to have it (the Color Palette Lit sample's do: copy them again).
- **An entrance can name its door** (`EntranceData.door`, layout key `door` on the entrance, written only when set; docs/EDITOR.md §6.7, `EntranceDoorTests`): a Storey Opening door's id, or `plain` for the built-in one. It has that door whatever its style picks for its wall (`OpeningSets.Placed`, with its entry's own recess where the style's list has it); naming none, or a door the project doesn't have, it is the style's pick as before. The baked layout format is version 8.
- **Where a building moved up and down stands, and a handle's place on it** (docs/EDITOR.md §6.11; `ElevationTests`): `Ground.Level`, level with the site's ground or the ground floor of a building within a metre of it where it comes within 15 cm of one (`Ground.LevelSnap`), otherwise to the centimetre, and the neighbour it is level with; `Geo.Deepest`, the point inside an outline furthest from its edges, to about a 1/500 of its size, from grids round the best.
- **Picking windows and street doors as the old BuildingGenerator** (`WindowPick.Generator`, layout value `generator`; docs/OPENINGS.md §7.1, `GeneratorPickTests`): by the edge, the storey and the bay alone, through its seed (edge·100003 + floor·1009 + tile; a door edge·100003) and `System.Random`'s first number in single precision (`OpeningSets.PickAsGenerator`, `SystemRandomFirst`, worked out on the stack; `WindowAsGenerator`, `DoorAt`), so a building it made gets the same window in every fixed bay and the same door. Its SimpleRed theme's ground floor got 2 windows of 8 and 0 of 9 on two walls where the old one had 4 and 3.
- **A door's hole bigger or smaller than the door, and closed recesses** (`FacadeStyle.doorRecessOffset`, `recessBack`, layout keys the same, written only when set; docs/EDITOR.md §6.7, `DoorHoleTests`), for the old BuildingGenerator's door inset offset and capped insets: a recessed artist's door on a shell storey sits in a hole that much bigger each side and over it (held to its bay with fixed bays, under the band), smaller hiding its frame behind the wall (`Opening.hl`, `hr`, `ht`: where the door is in its hole); and a recess's back in the wall's colour across the hole at its depth (`Facade.RecessBack`, LOD0 and LOD1). The old Brown theme's door showed its light frame round it, which the old generator's 21 cm smaller hole hid.
- **Hiding a building at run time, and a handle object for it** (`StoreySite.SetHidden(buildingId, hidden)`, `IsHidden`; `StoreyBuildingHandle`; docs/PLAY.md §6, `HiddenBuildingTests`), for a converted scene's own occlusion, which switches off the GameObject of the building the player is behind while its shadow and collider stay. Hidden, a building draws nothing in the colour pass or the depth prepass at any LOD, its massing in its merged cell too, and casts its shadow as before; its collider stays. Its state row's displayed LOD goes 4 past the LOD it shows (`Lod.StateRow`, `BuildingTable.SetHidden`; `STOREY_HIDDEN`, `StoreyHidden`, `StoreyShownLod` in StoreyOcclusion.hlsl), which the colour and depth passes draw none of and the shadow caster reads as the LOD; the rest of the row and every LOD write keep it, so the LOD manager, cross-fades, residency, cells and the steady variant carry on under it and showing it again gives back the row it would have had. Nothing per frame and no garbage: a building that isn't hidden costs a compare a vertex, and only a hidden one's vertices read their party walls' neighbours, whose LOD0 walls stay while the neighbour shows. Kept by id on the site, so it holds through loading over frames, edits and the site switched off and on. Storey Props go with their building from the colour pass and keep their shadows (StoreyProp.hlsl now defines `STOREY_SHADOW` in the shadow caster), and one drawn whole is switched off. Storey's own occlusion takes no hidden building for one in the way (`OcclusionCore.Hidden`) and shows no footprint for one hidden meanwhile. The handle (`site`, `buildingId`) hides its building in `OnDisable` and shows it in `OnEnable`, so a game system that switches a GameObject off hides that building; it draws its building's box while selected. With nothing hidden, everything draws as before.

Four more rules for an artist's facade detail on a wall: *Scattered on the walls* (brick patches: an even spread, a jittered grid rather than rejection sampling, so it neither clumps nor needs a try budget), *Along the top of the wall* and *Along the foot of the wall* (so many to a bay, never across a doorway) and *Up the corners* (stacked, facing out along the corner's bisector; standing out, set in, or both). - **Slice**, a third way an artist's window or door is fitted to its openings (docs/OPENINGS.md §4.3, docs/EDITOR.md §6.9), as 9-slice scaling fits a sprite: what lies between the slice lines stretches, and what lies outside them keeps its size and moves with the hole's sides. So one prefab fits every window type and width a style has, its shutters, sill, pediment and frame members unstretched. `OpeningKind.slice` and its borders (`borderL`, `borderR`, `borderB`, `borderT`: the lines in from the hole's sides), `StoreyOpening.slice` and `borders`, read from the Storey Cutout's `borders` for a prefab.

Objects that keep their shape aren't cut.

The style still decides where windows go and their sill's height. A street door keeps the 1.6 m doorway and takes its own height. It kept its size inside the style's hole, so the style's window width had to be set to match.
  - `OpeningKind.reachL`, `reachR`, `reachB`, `reachT`: how far LOD0 reaches past the hole, found when the kind is registered. `Opening.rl`, `rr`, `rb`, `rt` carry it as placed (scaled with a stretched window; `Facade.Dressing`), and `Left`, `Right`, `Bottom`, `Top` are the opening with its dressing.

A door's dressing narrows its own clamp. A window keeps 10 cm between its dressing and a door's (`Facade.NearDoor`); their holes still keep 30 cm.

- Colours of their own for the parts of artists' windows, doors and facade details (docs/OPENINGS.md §6, docs/COLOURS.md §3.9): `OpeningKind.Part.own`, `DetailKind.Part.own`, and the assets' `Part.own`, a palette colour the part keeps whatever the style, in place of its style colour (a window's glass always takes the style's). `KindColors` hands each such colour a slot from 24 up, for the session, which the part's vertices carry in their slot byte; the shaders already read slots 24 and up from `_StoreyDetailColors`, which `BuildingTable.SyncKindColors` now fills with the palette's indices, again when the palette invalidates them. The building's remap still applies. 232 colours for the project; past them a part takes its style colour (`KindColors.Refused`). `ColorResolver` resolves them for the census.
- Artist-made windows and doors from prefabs (docs/OPENINGS.md §3, docs/EDITOR.md §6.9), as Flamingo's old BuildingGenerator takes them: any objects with a MeshFilter and a MeshRenderer, each where it is in the prefab, nested prefabs and variants too. A Storey Opening's **Prefab** (`StoreyOpening.prefab`, `lod1Prefab`) is editor-only, as its mesh is, so neither the prefab nor its meshes and materials ship. Its parts are one for each material (`StoreyOpening.Part.key`, `label`), so they keep their colours when objects are added, removed or reordered. A door's leaf can be objects, not only a material.
  - **Storey Cutout** (`StoreyCutout`, *Add Component › Storey*) on the prefab's root is the hole: its size and middle in the root's X and Y, so the pivot can be anywhere, and where the wall's face is along Z. Without one, the meshes' bounds are the hole.
  - `KindBake` (engine-free) takes each object through its matrix into the opening's space (the root's scale applied, as Unity instantiates a prefab), turns a mirrored object the right way out, groups triangles into parts by key, welds what the model split, and leaves out the faces nobody sees: those inside the wall round the hole, and those on its faces turned into it. It counts the faces lying on the wall's outer face, facing out, which flicker. An asset made from a mesh is baked through it too; one baked before keeps its baked copy (`baked` 0) until it is baked again.
  - `OpeningKinds.Check`: a kind's triangles, the share of its hole left open as seen from the street, glass deeper than a shell storey's reveal (`ShellReveal`, 16.5 cm), anything behind the wall, and whether it has glass and a leaf. `Validate.KindTrial` tries a kind on a 20 × 12 m block of six storeys: its triangles at LOD0 and LOD1 against the generator's own, and the faces that overlap (`CoplanarCheck`), shell and walk-in.
- **Colliders At Load** on the Storey Site (`StoreySite.collidersAtLoad`, off by default; `SiteRenderer.AllColliders`, `MakeColliders`; docs/PLAY.md §5). In Play mode every building's collider is made as the site loads, before physics first steps, and kept for the session, so nothing placed on a building falls through it at the start, a Storey Prop or not. The layout then loads at once, not over frames. When the load ends, the collision meshes are made on the build threads at once (`CollisionMesh.BuildAll`), from a shape made for the purpose where a building has no LOD0, then cooked together and waited for; a building rebuilt by an edit gets its collider back in the same frame. Headless on four desktop cores, before PhysX's cooking: 155 ms for 300 buildings and 0.7 s for 3,000 (187 ms and 1.0 s walk-in), with 0.5 and 4.6 MB of collision meshes (3.7 and 22.6 MB walk-in). For a level's layout rather than a streamed city.
- `StoreySite.CollidersReadyAround` (docs/PLAY.md §5): every building within a radius of a point has its collider up, and true when the site makes none. A spawn or teleport of a physics character asks for detail there first (`PrefetchAround`) and waits for it behind its fade, as it waits for `DetailReadyAround`. `SiteRenderer.HasCollider` asks about one building, and `CollidersUpNear` round a point in site space.

- `TestCity.Options` (docs/CITY.md §1): a test city's count, the share of it walk-in (`WalkIn`, exact, from a random stream of its own, or the nearest the middle with `WalkInNearMiddle`; null for the prototype's mix), the seed, a height multiplier and cap (`HeightScale`, `MaxStoreys`, up to `TestCity.MostStoreys`, 60) and the storeys above which a walk-in building gets a lift (`LiftAbove`). - **Windows Painted From** on the quality asset (`LodSettings.WindowsPainted`, `PaintDistance`): where LOD0's windows start to clear, as a share of the LOD0 distance; beyond it they are painted, as LOD1's are. 0.7 on both tiers, and for a site without a quality asset: a LOD0 that comes late (built after the camera passed its switch distance, or out of view until the camera turned) still shows the painted windows when it appears, and they clear as the camera comes closer. They cleared from the switch itself (1), so such a LOD0's windows were see-through, or nearly, the moment it appeared, and the switch was plain to see. On an iPhone 11 Pro in landscape with a 60° field of view, LOD0 takes over at 54 m, and its windows now clear from 38 m to 23 m, where they did from 54 m to 33 m. The rooms behind them, drawn only within that distance, are left out for more buildings: 4–10 % fewer LOD0 colour-pass triangles at 0.5 on the walk-in test city's views (headless). **Windows Clear At** is a share of it.
- **Storey Shader Warm-Up** (`StoreyShaderWarmUp`, *Add Component ▸ Storey*): the pipeline states the city draws with, made before it first draws them (docs/PERFORMANCE.md §7.9). A state met for the first time mid-game is compiled in that frame. On Metal that is a hitch of milliseconds to tens of them, and the steady variant doubles the states a city can need. In a development build with **Trace** on, the component records the states a run uses with Unity's `GraphicsStateCollection`. It saves them when the app goes to the background or quits (`Storey <platform> <API>.graphicsstate` in the persistent data folder), and sends them to the editor when one is connected. Added to **Collections**, the collection for the device's platform and graphics API (and quality level) is made a few states a frame from the start, or all at once behind a loading screen. Works with the API in `UnityEngine.Experimental.Rendering` (6000.0 to 6000.4) and `UnityEngine.Rendering` (6000.5 on).
- `Documentation~/quality-tiers.md`: the URP settings to put beside each quality tier (rendering path, the GPU Resident Drawer, shadows, SSAO, HDR, MSAA, resolution, SH, stripping, warm-up), as starting points for the device runs to confirm.
- Profiler markers for the occlusion's halves, the props and the play kit (docs/PERFORMANCE.md §10.5), named as Storey's other stages are: "Storey occlusion: view and buildings in the way" and "Storey occlusion: cutaway" inside "Storey occlusion: frame" (`OcclusionCore.FrameView` and `FrameCutaway`, which `Frame` calls in turn), and "Storey props: frame". A finished build waits for its upload at 0.8 MB, not 3.0 (1.7, not 6.5, for a walk-in building). `MeshUpload.Upload(PackedMesh, …)`; `MeshUpload.Vertex` and `Cutaway` are now `PackedMesh.Vertex` and `Cutaway`.
- A LOD0 mesh past 65,535 vertices comes in parts with 16-bit indices, not as one mesh with 32-bit ones. The test city's 16 largest LOD0s take 8% less GPU memory (90.5 MB, was 98.6), in 51 meshes instead of 32.
- Building and welding a LOD0 allocates half as much: 14 MB instead of 29 for a building of the walk-in test city, on average. `MeshBuilder.W`, a copy of each vertex's wall data that nothing read since the walls' data moved into the building table, is gone; `MeshBuilder.Weld` works in place with a table of vertex indices instead of a dictionary of copied keys; boxes add their faces with `MeshBuilder.Quad`, without an array each. The meshes are the same to the bit.

The walk model's `ShapeOf`, `Use`, `ShapeCount` and `ShapesBuilt` replace `Lod0Of`, `UseLod0` and `Lod0Count` (docs/CITY.md §7).
- LOD1's walls are flat: a plain window is an opaque pane 3 cm proud of the wall (`Lod1.PaneProud`), its reveal shaded in the shader at the pane's sides and head (`StoreyRevealShade`), instead of a hole cut in the wall with modelled reveals. Doors and artist-made windows keep their openings. On the 3,000-building test city LOD1 goes from about 4,230 triangles a building to 980. `Lod1.Build(..., recessed: true)` builds the prototype's LOD1 for the parity tests.
- Every object a Storey Site makes (buildings, their LOD objects and colliders, the merged cells, Sink's footprints) is on the site's own layer, not Default; changing the site's layer moves them. `SiteRenderer.Layer`.
- `StoreyQualitySettings`' level-of-detail fields are now the automatic LOD's numbers (`lod0PixelsPerMetre`, `lod1PixelsPerMetre`, `lod2PixelsPerMetre`, `hysteresis`, `fadeSeconds`, `farDistance`, `lod0Residency`, `lod1Residency`, `buildBudgetMs`), with defaults from the prototype. `ToLodSettings`, `ApplyTo`.
- Cut corners are kept as one corner: `CornerData` (the building's and a setback's `corners`), and `CornerCuts` (`Sharp`, `Around`, `Cut`, `Clear`, `CutAll`, `ClearAll`). The outline still holds the cut's points. `CornerShape` moved to the `Triband.Storey` namespace.
- LOD0 vertices are 16 bytes smaller (32 bytes now, with the building tag). They carry only the cutaway kind and wall id; each wall's start and normal sit once in the building table (`_StoreyWallData`), and the shader reads them by id. On the demo street LOD0 goes from 4.6 to 3.1 MB.
- `MeshUpload.Upload` welds exact duplicate vertices (`MeshBuilder.Weld`, about 1% of LOD0's; every triangle is unchanged), and calls `Mesh.Optimize` when asked. `SiteRenderer` asks in Play mode, not for the editor's previews.
- `StoreyGlobals.SetOcclusion` takes the player's chest (world space) and the hole radius in metres. The shader projects the player itself, so Cutout and Fade hold on reversed-depth platforms and flipped render targets.
- A building's row in the building table is its index in the layout, so party walls find their neighbour in the shader.

- **Storey Diagnostics** (`StoreyDiagnostics`, *Add Component ▸ Storey*): a flight recorder for a device run with no debugger attached (from Windows, say, where there is no Xcode).
  - It keeps a line for each of the last frames: its time and, with Frame Timing Stats on, the CPU's and the GPU's; how many frames the GPU is behind (a 4-byte buffer read back each frame); the dynamic resolution's scale; each site's LOD numbers, the vertices of the LOD0 meshes shown and in view (`LodStats.lod0Vertices`, `lod0VerticesInView`) and wall ids in use; the building the player is in, with its floor and state row; the buildings in the way; the feet and the camera; memory in use (the process's, Unity's and the graphics driver's, as the Memory Profiler counts it). The frames before the first of those are kept in a section of their own.

When no frame has come for a second, it notes the stage the main thread is in and when the GPU last finished a frame. The stage is Storey's own (`StoreyTimings.Stage`: the innermost timing scope open, and the building it is for) or physics, scripts or rendering.
  - The next run shows the record with a Copy button. `StoreyDiagnostics.FilePath`, `Note`, `Recording`; `BuildingTable.WallsInUse`.
  - On iOS it notes Metal's own word on each GPU command buffer that fails, which a player without Xcode attached reports nowhere: the error's code and description (Metal's reason, such as a GPU address fault, or more geometry than a pass's memoryless attachments allow), the frame it was made in, and each of its passes' state (faulted, affected, completed) with their labels. An Objective-C++ plug-in for iOS only does it (`Unity/Plugins/iOS/StoreyMetalErrors.mm`). It hooks the Metal command queue class's three command-buffer methods, so every command buffer Unity makes gets a completion handler and asks Metal for its encoders' states on an error. A note a few seconds in says how many it has seen complete. **Metal Errors** turns it off (`StoreyDiagnostics.metalErrors`), and the frame line counts the failures (`mtl-fail`).
  - It notes every camera that renders and each change: its target (the screen, or a texture with its size, depth bits, MSAA and memoryless mode), its culling mask and URP's per-camera settings (render type, renderer, post-processing, depth and opaque textures, anti-aliasing, shadows). The header lists the pipeline's renderers: rendering path, depth priming, when the depth texture is copied, the intermediate texture mode and the renderer features.
